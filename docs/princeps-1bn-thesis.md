# PRINCEPS — Path to £1B

## The Thesis

Energy predevelopment is broken. Developers spend £60-160k and 3-6 months per project on fragmented consultant reports, manual grid studies, and disconnected spreadsheet models — before a single panel is installed. 78% of that cost is information gathering across siloed tools and data sources that don't talk to each other.

**Princeps fixes this by creating a full-stack asset planning OS, automating feasibility assessments, regulatory compliance and legal reports, all in the same platform as financial modelling, GIS simulation, and grid connection calculations.**

Every solar farm, wind project, BESS installation, and data centre built in the UK in the next decade will touch this platform. Not because we're the best at any single analysis — but because we're the only platform where site selection, grid engineering, environmental screening, planning intelligence, financial modelling, and document generation all share the same data context. The result that took a consultant team 4 months is generated in 60 seconds.

## The Two Arcs

### ARC 1: Full-Stack Asset Planning OS (Months 0-18)

Arc 1 is about capturing the two fastest-growing and highest-value segments of UK energy infrastructure — **battery energy storage and data centres** — then expanding into solar and wind as the platform matures.

BESS and data centres are the primary targets for three reasons. First, they are the fastest-growing asset classes: BESS operational capacity is scaling from 5.6 GW to 27 GW by 2030 (a 5x increase in 5 years), while UK data centre demand has 50 GW in the grid connection queue and construction spend is rising from £1.75B to £10B/year by 2029. Second, they have the most acute predevelopment pain: BESS developers need revenue stacking analysis (frequency response + capacity market + arbitrage + wholesale) that no single consultant provides, and DC developers need co-location analysis with behind-the-meter energy assets that no existing tool can model. Third, they command the highest ACV: a DC developer pays £120-300k/year for a platform that replaces £200-500k of consultant fees per project, while a BESS developer pays £18-30k/year to replace £30-60k per project.

The platform serves all asset types from day one because the underlying engineering is shared — grid connection assessment uses the same 6 DNO databases whether the project is a 50MW solar farm or a 200MW data centre, and the planning ML model trained on 13,995 REPD decisions doesn't discriminate by technology. But the go-to-market is deliberately sequenced: BESS and DC first (highest urgency, highest ACV, least served by existing tools), then solar and wind (larger volume, lower ACV, more competitive tooling landscape).

**The BESS opportunity is immediate and underserved.** There are 2,533 BESS projects in the REPD pipeline totalling 143 GW. These developers face a unique challenge: unlike solar or wind, BESS revenue depends entirely on market dispatch — frequency response contracts, capacity market auctions, wholesale arbitrage, and embedded benefits. A BESS feasibility study requires not just grid connection analysis but revenue stacking simulation, degradation modelling, and market timing analysis. No existing platform combines these with site selection and grid engineering. Princeps does. The approximately 150 active BESS developers in the UK each manage 5-20 projects, spending £30-60k per project on fragmented consultant engagements. At £18-30k ACV, Princeps captures this spend at 50-70% displacement.

**The data centre opportunity is the highest-value vertical in energy infrastructure.** The UK has 50 GW of data centre demand in the grid connection queue, driven by AI compute expansion. Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Equinix, and Vantage are all actively acquiring sites for new facilities. Their predevelopment requirements are uniquely complex: they need grid connection analysis at 100-500MW scale, 24/7 carbon-free energy matching (not annual — hourly), behind-the-meter energy co-siting, cooling efficiency modelling against local climate, water stress assessment, and Uptime Institute Tier compliance. No existing tool addresses this combination. Princeps is the only platform that co-optimises data centre siting with energy generation and battery storage using real physics engines (HPE SustainDC thermal model, OpenDC power curves). The approximately 40 UK DC developers each manage 3-10 projects at £200-500k predevelopment spend per project. At £120-300k ACV, a single DC customer generates as much revenue as 20 solar developers.

**Solar and wind developers are the volume play that scales the business after BESS and DC establish the platform.** There are 7,023 solar projects (63.7 GW) and approximately 3,000 wind projects (175 GW onshore + offshore) in the pipeline. These are the largest developer populations — roughly 400 solar companies and 120 wind companies. They each manage larger portfolios (5-30 projects) at lower per-project spend (£40-120k). At £6-30k ACV they provide the customer volume that drives the network effects: more users generating more planning data, more grid queue intelligence, and more comparable transaction data. Solar and wind developers are targeted from month 3 onwards, after BESS and DC design partners have validated the core platform.

**The total addressable market across all four asset classes is concrete and measurable.** There are 13,995 energy infrastructure projects in the UK REPD pipeline totalling 372 GW, plus 50 GW of DC demand in the grid queue. Every one of these projects spent or will spend £60-160k on predevelopment. The 238 GW retained in the reformed NESO grid queue (December 2025) represents projects that have already committed capital to feasibility studies. Behind them are thousands more at the site identification stage. The UK has approximately 1,230 companies that actively develop, invest in, or consult on energy infrastructure. This is derived from cross-referencing REPD operator counts, industry trade bodies and associations, and Companies House SIC code 35110-35140 registrations.

**The revenue calculation reflects the BESS-and-DC-first sequencing.** In the first 6 months, the majority of revenue comes from 5-10 BESS developers (£18-30k ACV each) and 2-3 DC developers (£120-300k ACV each). By month 12, 15 BESS accounts and 5 DC accounts contribute £720k ARR — 72% of the £1M ARR target — while 50 solar/wind developers on Professional tier contribute the remaining £300k. By month 18, the mix shifts as solar/wind volume grows: BESS accounts at £1.2M, DC accounts at £1.0M, and solar/wind at £1.3M, totalling £3.5M ARR across 225 customers. The DC vertical never exceeds 30% of revenue but consistently drives the highest per-account economics.

The developer's ROI is immediate regardless of asset class. A BESS developer running 10 projects per year currently spends £300-600k on predevelopment consultants. Princeps Enterprise at £30k/year delivers equivalent output for 90-95% less. A DC developer spending £200-500k per project on feasibility studies replaces that with a £300k/year platform subscription that covers unlimited projects. They don't need a business case — they need a demo. This is why conversion from free trial to paid targets 8-12%, above the 5% B2B SaaS average: the value is demonstrated in the first 60 seconds of use.

**By month 12, the model projects 91 paying customers generating £1.0M ARR.** This assumes 3 design partner customers in month 1 growing to 18 new sign-ups per month by month 12, with 2% monthly churn offset by tier upgrades as developers expand from Professional (£499/month) to Enterprise (£2,499/month). ### ARC 2: Lifecycle Platform + European Expansion (Months 18-48)

Arc 2 is about two things: extending downstream along the asset lifecycle (construction, operations, transactions) and replicating the platform across European markets. Both are natural expansions that require no fundamental technology change — only new integrations and localisation.

**The lifecycle expansion is driven by customer pull, not product push.** A developer who used Princeps for predevelopment has their entire project dataset in the platform — grid connection data, financial model, planning documents, environmental screening, G99 application. When they move to construction, they don't want to export that data to Procore and re-enter it. They want Princeps to track the EPC tender, the construction timeline, and the commissioning checklist against the same site context. When the asset is operational, they want Princeps to compare actual generation against the PvWatts prediction they relied on during investment committee. When they sell the asset 5 years later, they want Princeps to generate the data room with every document, analysis, and compliance record from the project's entire history.

This is why net revenue retention targets 115-130%. Each developer account naturally expands as their projects progress through the lifecycle. A £6,000 Professional account that started with site screening becomes a £30,000 Enterprise account when they add grid connection and financial modelling, then a £120,000 Portfolio account when they bring their full pipeline of 20 projects onto the platform, then adds £36,000 of Construction Intelligence per project, then £60,000/year of Operational Analytics per operating asset. A single developer relationship that started at £6k can compound to £200k+ within 3 years without any price increases — purely from product expansion within their existing workflow.

**The European expansion is a replication exercise, not a reinvention.** Every EU country has the same predevelopment bottleneck — grid connection queues, planning permission, environmental screening, financial modelling. The engineering calculations are identical (power flow physics doesn't change at borders). What changes is the data sources: France has RTE and Enedis instead of NESO and NGED, Germany has the Bundesnetzagentur, the Netherlands has TenneT. Each country requires 2-3 months of data integration work to connect the local grid operator, planning portal, and environmental constraint databases. The product, pricing, and go-to-market are the same.

The European energy infrastructure pipeline is approximately 5x the UK's. The EU Green Deal and REPowerEU are driving solar capacity from 260 GW to 750 GW by 2030, onshore wind from 190 GW to 510 GW, and offshore wind from 16 GW to 111 GW. Germany alone has a 400 GW energy pipeline. This is a £5.5-19B TAM that can be addressed by localising the same platform.

**The financial trajectory from month 18 to month 48 builds on Arc 1's foundation.** Starting from 225 customers and £3.49M ARR at month 18, the model projects growth to 1,900 customers and £72M ARR by month 48 (Q4 2029). This growth is driven by three compounding factors: new customer acquisition accelerating from 33/month to 100/month as the sales team scales from 0 to 15 account executives; existing customer expansion through lifecycle modules increasing blended ACV from £15k to £38k; and European market entry contributing 400 customers at an average £9k ACV by year 4.

The key milestones in Arc 2 are: Series A raise at month 12-15 (£5M at £25M pre-money, triggered by £1M ARR and 91 customers); first Portfolio tier customers at month 15; DC vertical beta with 2 hyperscalers at month 21; Ireland and Netherlands pilot at month 24; Series B raise at month 27 (£20M at £100M pre-money, triggered by £9.6M ARR and 480 customers); Construction Intelligence module launch at month 30; Operational Analytics module at month 33; and Series C or pre-IPO round at month 42 (£50M at £500M+ pre-money, triggered by £56M ARR and 1,600 customers).

**The revenue mix at month 48 shows a mature multi-product business.** Professional subscriptions contribute £6M (8%), Enterprise contributes £15M (21%), Portfolio contributes £24M (33%), the DC vertical contributes £9M (13%), Construction Intelligence contributes £4.3M (6%), Operational Analytics contributes £6M (8%), the Transaction Platform contributes £4M (6%), and European revenue contributes £3.7M (5%). No single product line exceeds 33% of revenue, and no single customer exceeds 2% — this is a diversified, defensible revenue base.

**The valuation at £72M ARR depends on the multiple, which depends on growth rate, gross margin, and net revenue retention.** At 12x revenue — conservative for vertical SaaS with 85% gross margin and 115% NRR — the valuation is £864M. At 15x — justified by 80%+ year-over-year growth and multi-product expansion — the valuation is £1.08B. Benchmarked against Enverus (acquired by Blackstone at 12-13x on $500M+ ARR in August 2025), Aurora Solar (15-30x on $135M ARR), and Planet Labs (38-47x on $244M ARR), a 12-15x multiple on £72M ARR puts Princeps at **£860M-£1.1B** — comfortably in billion-pound territory.

Even the bear case is fundable. At 60% of the base case growth rate, Princeps reaches £28M ARR with 800 customers by month 48. At 8x revenue (distressed multiple), that's still a £224M outcome — a strong Series B/C company. The bull case, at 140% of base growth, reaches £120M ARR and £1.44B valuation. The range is £224M to £1.44B, with the base case at £864M and a growth-premium scenario exceeding £1B.
